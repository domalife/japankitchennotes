Japan Kitchen Notes

What the words on a Japanese tea bag or knife box actually mean.

What this is

Japanese tea and kitchen tools are sold abroad with the original vocabulary left on the label: gyokuro, fukamushi, shirogami, ginsan. The words are precise and they describe real differences, but the explanations are mostly written in Japanese and stay there.

These notes take that material and set it out plainly in English: comparison tables, short reference lists, and the one or two facts that actually change what you should buy.

What is covered

  • Tea — sencha, gyokuro, matcha and hojicha; shading time; water temperature; steaming depth; growing regions; cultivars; what a matcha label can and cannot claim
  • Knives — santoku, gyuto, nakiri and petty; white, blue and stainless steels; single and double bevel; where knives are made and how that changes them
  • Sharpening and care — which whetstone grit to buy first, and how to keep carbon steel from rusting
Everything here is checkable. Where a claim is a matter of taste or marketing rather than fact, it is labelled as such — for example, "ceremonial grade" matcha is a marketing term, not a regulated standard.
